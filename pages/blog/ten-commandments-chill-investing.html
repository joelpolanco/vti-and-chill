The 10 Commandments of Chill Investing

Moses came down from the mountain with two stone tablets. We're coming down from our spreadsheets with ten investing commandments — the core truths of building wealth without losing your mind.

The 30-Second Version

If you only have 30 seconds, here is the whole argument. The rest of the post is the receipts.

  • Start now, not later. The person who started at 25 has more than twice the wealth of the person who started at 35 — same contributions, different clocks.
  • Pay yourself first, automatically. The first transfer on payday goes to investments, before anything else. Automation removes willpower from the equation.
  • Costs are the only return variable you fully control. A 1% fee difference on a $500K portfolio can cost $250,000+ over 20 years.
  • Stay in the market through every crash. Every major crash in history — 1929, 1987, 2000, 2008, 2020 — was followed by recovery to new all-time highs.
  • Simplicity beats complexity. One to three low-cost index funds outperforms most complicated portfolios over time, after fees and behavioral errors.

Print these out. Tape them to your monitor. Read them the next time the market drops 15% and your group chat is on fire.

Commandment I
Thou Shalt Start Now, Not When Conditions Are Perfect

The perfect time to start investing was yesterday. The second best time is today.

There will never be a moment when the market looks safe enough, your income feels certain enough, or your knowledge feels complete enough to take the first step. If you're waiting for perfect conditions, you're waiting forever. The person who invested $1,000 per month for 30 years starting at 25 has more than twice the wealth of someone who waited until 35 to start the same routine — not because of different strategies, but because of time.

Start with whatever you have. Start with $50 if that's what's available. Start imperfectly. The cost of waiting compounds just as surely as returns do.

Commandment II
Thou Shalt Pay Thyself First

Not what's left over. First.

Saving what remains after spending is a guaranteed way to have nothing left to save. Every paycheck, the very first transfer is to your investment accounts. Before the rent, before the groceries, before the streaming services — before you spend a dollar, you invest your predetermined amount.

Automation is the execution mechanism for this commandment. Set up automatic contributions that fire on payday. The money you never see in your checking account is the money you'll never miss. Automated investing is the closest thing to a guaranteed wealth-building system that exists.

Commandment III
Thou Shalt Keep Costs Brutally Low

Every dollar you pay in fees is a dollar that doesn't compound. And compounding amplifies everything — including the damage from fees.

A 1% annual advisory fee sounds harmless. On a $500,000 portfolio, it's $5,000 per year. Over 20 years, with compounding, that 1% fee can cost you $250,000 or more in foregone growth. Vanguard's VTI has an expense ratio of 0.03%. Avantis funds run around 0.15–0.25%. These are the appropriate benchmarks for investment costs. Anything significantly above these numbers requires extraordinary justification.

The index fund revolution democratized investing by eliminating the toll booth between your savings and market returns. Walk through the toll-free gate.

Commandment IV
Thou Shalt Not Try to Time the Market

You cannot reliably predict when the market will rise or fall. Nobody can. Not the hedge fund managers, not the CNBC pundits, not the cousin who made money on Bitcoin that one time.

Studies of investors who tried to time the market — moving to cash before drops, reinvesting before rises — consistently show they underperform the strategy of simply staying invested. The reason is mathematical: missing the market's best days is catastrophic. About half of the S&P 500's best days historically occurred within two weeks of the worst days. The person waiting on the sidelines missed both.

Your job isn't to predict the market. Your job is to be in it, consistently, for decades.

Commandment V
Thou Shalt Stay the Course in the Chaos

When markets drop 30%, every instinct you have will scream at you to sell. The news will be dark. Smart-sounding people will say this time is different. The forums will fill with fear.

Stay. Invested.

Every major market crash in history — 1929, 1987, 2000, 2008, 2020 — was followed by a recovery that produced new all-time highs. Every single one. The investors who bailed during the panic locked in losses and missed the recovery. The investors who did nothing — or better, bought more — came out ahead.

Volatility is the price of admission for equity returns. The ride is uncomfortable. The destination is worth it.

The boring path is the winning path

Survivorship bias creates the illusion that everyone is getting rich from speculation except you. They're not. Studies of retail trading accounts consistently show that active traders, as a group, dramatically underperform buy-and-hold index investors after accounting for taxes, fees, and the spectacular losses that don't make it into the cocktail party story. Let others chase. You compound.

Commandment VI
Thou Shalt Not Covet Thy Neighbor's Hot Stock

Someone at your office made money on a meme stock. Someone in your Discord found a "guaranteed" crypto play. Your brother-in-law day-trades and he was up 40% last quarter.

These stories have two things in common: you hear about the wins, and you don't hear about the losses. Loss aversion plus survivorship bias creates the illusion that everyone is getting rich from speculation except you. They're not. Studies of retail trading accounts consistently show that active traders, as a group, dramatically underperform buy-and-hold index investors after accounting for taxes, fees, and the spectacular losses that don't make it into the cocktail party story.

The boring path is the winning path. Let others chase. You compound.

Commandment VII
Thou Shalt Automate Everything Possible

The fewer financial decisions you make on an ongoing basis, the better your outcomes. Not because you're bad at decisions — because every investment decision is an opportunity to make an emotional one.

Automate your 401(k) contribution increases (most plans let you set automatic annual increases). Automate your IRA contributions. Automate your taxable brokerage contributions. Automate dividends to reinvest. When the system runs without your intervention, you remove your future panicking self from the equation.

The best investment strategy is the one you stick to. Automation is what makes sticking to it frictionless.

Commandment VIII
Thou Shalt Honor Thy Tax-Advantaged Accounts

The 401(k), IRA, HSA, and Roth IRA are legal gift-wrapped packages from the government. They let your investments grow tax-deferred or tax-free, which is a compounding advantage that applies every year for decades.

The order of operations: employer 401(k) match first (it's free money, there is no excuse), then HSA if you're eligible (triple tax advantaged, unbeatable for healthcare and stealth retirement savings), then Roth IRA if you're under the income limits, then back to maxing the 401(k).

Only after all of these should you be adding significant money to a taxable brokerage account. You are leaving substantial money on the table if you're contributing to a taxable account while tax-advantaged space sits unused.

Commandment IX
Thou Shalt Not Confuse Complexity With Sophistication

The financial services industry profits from your belief that investing is complicated. Complexity means you need an advisor. Complexity means you'll buy the wrong thing without guidance. Complexity means there's an ongoing reason to pay fees.

The dirty secret: the most effective portfolio for most people is also the simplest. One to three low-cost index funds. Automatic contributions. Rebalance annually. The overwhelming evidence from decades of academic research and real investor outcomes supports this not despite its simplicity but because of it.

A complicated portfolio isn't a sophisticated portfolio. It's just expensive and harder to maintain. Add complexity only when there's specific, evidence-backed reason to do so — not because it feels more serious. JL Collins has made this case more compellingly than anyone: own the whole market, keep costs low, stay the course.

Commandment X
Thou Shalt Measure Thy Wealth in Freedom, Not Balances

This is the commandment that changes how you experience the whole journey.

Your portfolio balance is not your goal. Your portfolio balance is an instrument. What it buys is the ability to choose how you spend your time — to work on what you want to work on, to say no to what you don't want, to have options in a world where most people have none.

Track your savings rate. Track your FIRE number and your progress toward it. Notice each year when your investment income covers more of your expenses. The day your portfolio generates enough to cover your annual spending is the day work becomes optional. That's the destination. The balance is just the odometer.

Build wealth so you can build a life. Not the other way around.

The Bottom Line

Ten commandments. One philosophy. Start early, invest simply, keep costs low, automate everything, and stay calm when everyone else is panicking. Let your money do the work while you do the living.

These principles are not complicated. They are not exclusive. They are available to anyone willing to stop gambling and start building. Engrave them somewhere you'll see them on the days the market reminds you that it's never a smooth ride.

Chill is not laziness. Chill is confidence backed by evidence.
